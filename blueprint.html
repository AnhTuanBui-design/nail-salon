Inspiration

Design direction

Taken from the Pedi N Nails Dundas East site you shared. Borrow the mood and the layout patterns, and make the name, photos, wording and shade names your own. The prompts below already ask the AI to do this.

Mood

Quiet and editorial, like a magazine spread, not a busy promo page. The site sells an hour of calm, not only a set of nails.

Linenbackground
Sandalt sections
EspressoBook buttons
Taupebody text
Muted goldlines, arrows
  • Headlines: tall, high-contrast serif in a light weight, left-aligned, short and confident.
  • Labels: small uppercase with wide spacing, preceded by a short thin line.
  • Body: clean sans-serif, muted color, lots of air.
  • Buttons: square corners, solid espresso for Book, outlined for secondary.
  • Photos: close-up hands, soft light, warm tones. Real work only.

Layout patterns worth borrowing

  • Full-bleed hero with a soft dark overlay and a two-line headline about the result and the experience.
  • Category strip under the hero: small icons for Manicure, Pedicure, Enhancements, Nail Art, Waxing.
  • Menu as numbered rows (01, 02…) with thin dividers and "From $X →". No cards.
  • Uneven gallery: one large photo beside smaller ones, each with a one-line caption.
  • The salon room: photos of the space with short captions, plus opening hours.
  • Shade picker: named colors to choose before arriving.
  • Journal: short guides (gel vs. polish, nail shapes) that bring in Google traffic.
  • Visit block per location: address, phone, hours, directions, Book.
  • Header: simple nav, an E-Gift Cards link, and a solid Book Now button.

Pages

What each page does

Phase 1 launchPhase 2 growthPhase 3 loyalty

Home

Phase 1

Make a visitor want to book within five seconds.

  • Hero photo, one-line pitch, Book Now button
  • Popular services with "from $" prices
  • Gallery preview, 6–8 recent sets
  • Google rating and 3–5 review quotes
  • Promo banner (e.g. first visit 20% off)
  • Hours, address, map link, tap-to-call

Services & Pricing

Phase 1

Answer "what do you offer and how much?" without a phone call.

  • Grouped by category with jump links
  • Name, description, duration, price for each
  • Add-ons listed separately
  • Book this button that pre-selects the service

Book Online

Phase 1

The main goal of the whole site. Turn visitors into appointments.

  • Choose services, technician, date and time
  • Only real open slots are shown
  • Contact details, notes, optional deposit
  • Confirmation plus SMS/email reminders
  • Self-serve cancel and reschedule links

Gallery

Phase 1

Show real work. This is what sells nail services.

  • Photo grid with style filters (French, chrome, ombré, art, acrylic, seasonal)
  • Tap to enlarge, tagged with the technician
  • Book this look button
  • Link to Instagram

Contact & Location

Phase 1

Help people find you and reach you fast.

  • Address with "Get directions" link
  • Phone, text/chat link, email
  • Hours, holiday hours, parking notes
  • Simple contact form

Our Team

Phase 2

Let clients pick the person whose style they like.

  • Photo, name, specialties, experience
  • 3–6 work samples each
  • Book with [name] button

Gift Cards

Phase 2

Extra revenue, especially around holidays.

  • Choose an amount or a service package
  • Emailed to the recipient with a message and date
  • Balance check and redemption code

FAQ & Policies

Phase 2

Cut down on repeat questions and no-shows.

  • Cancellation, late-arrival and deposit policy
  • How long gel, dip and acrylic last; aftercare
  • Payment methods, kids, accessibility

About Us

Phase 2

Build trust, especially about cleanliness.

  • Salon story and atmosphere photos
  • Sanitation standards (sterilized tools, single-use files, ventilation)
  • Product brands you use

Shade Picker

Phase 2

Let clients choose a color at home, so the appointment starts faster.

  • Named shades grouped by family and finish
  • Tap for a large preview and "Book with this shade"
  • Note that in-salon polish may differ slightly

Journal

Phase 2

Answer common questions and rank on Google for them.

  • Short guides with a category label and read time
  • Each post ends with related services and a Book button
  • Start with 3 posts, add one a month

Promotions & Account

Phase 3

Bring clients back every 2–3 weeks.

  • First-visit, birthday and referral offers
  • Loyalty points (e.g. 200 points = $10 off)
  • Customer login: upcoming and past bookings

How booking works

The same flow applies whether you embed Fresha or Square or build your own. If you build it yourself, every step and rule here is code you have to write and test.

Across the site

Site-wide features and the admin side

Every page

  • Mobile-first. Most salon visitors are on a phone.
  • Sticky Book Now button; fixed bottom bar on mobile with Call + Book.
  • Tap-to-call and chat (SMS, Zalo, WhatsApp or Messenger).
  • Reviews pulled from Google, with a link to leave one.
  • Local SEO: Google Business Profile, LocalBusiness schema, city in page titles.
  • Fast images: compressed, lazy-loaded gallery.
  • Accessibility: alt text, readable contrast, keyboard navigation.
  • Analytics with "booking started" and "booking completed" events.
  • Privacy policy and a cookie notice if you use tracking.

Admin (staff only)

  • Calendar: day/week view per technician, drag to reschedule, add walk-ins, mark no-shows.
  • Services: edit names, prices, durations without touching code.
  • Staff: working hours, days off, which services each person does.
  • Customers: visit history and notes (allergies, preferred shape and shade).
  • Gallery: upload from a phone, tag by style and technician.
  • Gift cards & promos: create codes, track balances.
  • Reports: revenue, bookings per tech, top services, no-show rate.

If you use Fresha, Square, Vagaro or Booksy, most of this already comes with it. You only build the gallery manager.

Only if you build booking yourself

                deposit_amount, notes, source (web|phone|walk_in)
AppointmentItem appointment_id, service_id | addon_id, price_charged

Step 2

Choosing your AI builder

All three routes work. The big decision is whether to embed an existing salon booking system or build your own. Embedding is faster, cheaper and far more reliable.

RouteExample toolsGood forBookingEffort
No-code AI site builderWix, Framer, Hostinger AI builderOwner who wants it live this week, no code at allEmbed Fresha / Square / Vagaro / Booksy1–3 days
AI app builderLovable, Bolt, v0Custom look, you describe it and the AI writes the code, one-click hostingEmbed a provider, or build your own with Supabase1–3 weeks
AI coding assistantClaude Code, CursorFull control and ownership of the code, easiest to grow laterEmbed, or custom with Next.js + Supabase + Stripe + Twilio2–6 weeks

Recommendation: use an AI app builder or Claude Code for the website, and embed a salon booking system such as Fresha or Square. Put your effort into photos, services and local SEO, which are what bring clients in. You can replace the booking system with your own later.
